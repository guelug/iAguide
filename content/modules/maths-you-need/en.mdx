## Attention, as a picture

<Term id="attention">Attention</Term> is a mixing step. For each token, the model builds a **query**. Every token (in the window) offers a **key** and a **value**. Scores say how much this query cares about each key; values are blended with those scores.

A sketch, not a paper:

$$
\mathrm{Attention}(Q,K,V) = \mathrm{softmax}\!\left(\frac{QK^\top}{\sqrt{d}}\right) V
$$

$d$ is the key dimension; the square root is a scaling hack so the scores do not explode as $d$ grows. Multi-head means several of these in parallel, then concatenated. GQA / MQA share keys and values across heads to shrink the KV cache — that is an engineering diagram, same idea.

You do not need to derive this to use a model. You need: **every new token attends over the cached keys**, which is why the KV cache exists.

## Softmax

<Term id="softmax">Softmax</Term> turns a vector of scores $z$ into a distribution:

$$
\mathrm{softmax}(z)_i = \frac{e^{z_i}}{\sum_j e^{z_j}}
$$

Big score → mass near 1. Temperature in sampling is (intuitively) dividing the logits before this step: $z / T$. $T \to 0$ is greedy.

## Cross-entropy

Training next-token is “be less surprised by the true next token.” <Term id="cross-entropy">Cross-entropy</Term> against a one-hot (or a label id) is the usual loss. Low loss on a token means the model already assigned it high probability. It is not a moral score.

If you LoRA on a tiny set until loss is ~0, you memorised. That can be what you wanted (a format) or a disaster (a name).

## Gradients, one paragraph

<VisualSlot id="maths-you-need:2" />

A <Term id="gradient">gradient</Term> is the direction to move parameters to reduce loss. Backprop computes it from the loss backward through the graph. An optimizer (AdamW, etc.) takes a step. You will almost never write this by hand. You will hit **NaNs** (LR too high, bad data) and **overfit** (too many steps). Those are gradient stories in the wild.

## LoRA as a low-rank delta

<VisualSlot id="maths-you-need:3" />

A linear layer is $y = W x$ with $W$ a big matrix. LoRA freezes $W$ and learns

$$
\Delta W = BA
$$

where $B$ is $d \times r$, $A$ is $r \times k$, and $r$ (the <Term id="rank">rank</Term>) is small. The served layer is $W + \frac{\alpha}{r} BA$ (scale conventions vary; follow your trainer).

Why it fits a GPU: you store two thin rectangles instead of a square. Why it can fail: if the behaviour you want is not low-rank in that layer, $r$ is too small.

## Case — temperature as a moral knob

Someone set temperature to 0 “to be truthful.” Softmax got sharper; the wrong token stayed wrong. Temperature is a distribution knob, not a conscience.
