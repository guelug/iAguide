## Similarity is a score, not a proof

<VisualSlot id="embeddings-and-retrieval:3" />

An <Term id="embedding">embedding</Term> is a list of numbers. Nearby lists *tend* to mean related things. Passage embeddings are what most “vector search” stacks store. Cosine similarity ranks candidates. It does not certify that the chunk answers the question.

Chunking is a product decision: too big and you retrieve noise; too small and you lose the sentence that had the number. Hybrid search (keyword + vector) and a reranker are ordinary, not exotic.

<VisualSlot id="embeddings-and-retrieval:4" caption="Too big drowns the number; too small splits it at the cut. A 10–20% overlap is the strip that lets the sentence survive." />

## Three ways to use a corpus

1. **Stuff** — paste the wiki into the prompt. Simple. Burns the window. Cache-hostile when the blob changes.
2. **Retrieve-then-read** — always run the retriever, stuff the top-k, generate. Classic RAG. The model never decides *whether* to fetch.
3. **Agentic loop** — the model is given a retrieve tool (and maybe others) and may skip it, retry a better query, or use the web instead.

<VisualSlot caption="Stuffing the corpus, retrieving top-k, or letting the agent choose a tool. Same files; different control flow." />

Naive RAG disappoints when the question needs two hops, when the chunk boundary split the answer, or when the retrieved text is instructions in disguise (see safety).

The particularity of *how* LlamaIndex or smolagents wire this sits in [LlamaIndex](/en/m/llamaindex) and [agentic RAG](/en/m/agentic-rag). Here we only need the three geometries.

## Case — HarborHR’s stuffed handbook

<VisualSlot id="embeddings-and-retrieval:2" />

HarborHR pasted a 40-page handbook into every turn. Prefill was 9 seconds. When legal updated *one* paragraph, the prefix cache died and the model still cited the old leave policy from a neighbouring chunk. They did not have a retrieval bug. They had a stuffing policy.
